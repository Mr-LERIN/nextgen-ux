Apple Mac mini M4: The Tiny Mac That Might Be More Powerful Than You Need

There is something almost ridiculous about the latest Mac mini. Small enough to fit in one hand, yet packed with serious M4 performance.

There is something almost ridiculous about the latest Mac mini. The fact that it is small enough to fit comfortably in one hand, weighs less than a kilogram, and looks more like a compact accessory than a serious computer. Yet inside that tiny aluminium box is Apple’s M4 chip, a 10-core CPU, a 10-core GPU, a 16-core Neural Engine, and enough performance to handle everything from everyday productivity to coding, creative work, AI applications, and even demanding video workflows.

This is the Mac mini M4 — and it may be one of the most interesting desktop computers Apple has made in years.

Smaller Than Ever

Apple completely redesigned the Mac mini for the M4 generation. The footprint is now just 5 inches by 5 inches, with a height of 2 inches. The M4 model weighs approximately 1.5 pounds, while the M4 Pro version comes in at about 1.6 pounds.

That is remarkably small for a desktop computer, but the compact design isn’t just about aesthetics. The Mac mini is essentially a small computer that lets you build the rest of your setup around it. Bring your own monitor, keyboard, mouse, speakers and storage, connect everything, and you’ve got a complete Mac desktop without paying for components you may already own.

And unlike a MacBook, you’re not carrying around a screen and keyboard that you don’t need.

The M4 Is the Real Story

The biggest reason the Mac mini is interesting isn’t its size. It’s what’s inside.

The standard M4 configuration comes with a 10-core CPU made up of four performance cores and six efficiency cores, alongside a 10-core GPU. It also includes hardware-accelerated ray tracing and a 16-core Neural Engine.

Apple gives the M4 120GB/s of memory bandwidth, while the M4 Pro pushes that figure to 273GB/s.

In practical terms, this means the Mac mini isn’t simply designed for browsing the web and writing documents. It can comfortably sit in the middle of a serious workstation.

Developers can run multiple applications and development environments. Designers can work with demanding creative software. Video editors can take advantage of Apple’s dedicated media engines. And people experimenting with AI tools can benefit from the combination of CPU, GPU and Neural Engine performance.

The M4 also supports hardware acceleration for H.264, HEVC, ProRes, ProRes RAW and AV1 decoding, making the machine particularly interesting for people working with video.

And Then There’s the M4 Pro

For people who want more than the standard M4 can offer, Apple has the M4 Pro.

The M4 Pro starts with a 12-core CPU and 16-core GPU, with configurations reaching up to a 14-core CPU and 20-core GPU. It also supports up to 48GB of unified memory in Apple’s current configurations.

This is where the Mac mini starts becoming a genuinely serious workstation. The M4 Pro also brings Thunderbolt 5, with transfer speeds of up to 120Gb/s. Apple says the machine can drive up to three 6K displays, making it possible to build an impressive multi-monitor workstation around a computer that is barely larger than a coaster.

A Desktop That Doesn’t Look Like a Desktop

One of the best things about the Mac mini is how little space it demands. Traditional desktop PCs usually mean a large tower sitting underneath or beside your desk. The Mac mini takes a completely different approach.

You can put it beside your monitor. You can hide it behind your display. You can place it underneath your desk. You can even carry it between different workspaces. The computer itself essentially disappears into your setup.

And that is part of Apple’s appeal with the Mac mini. Instead of selling you an all-in-one computer, Apple gives you the core computer and lets you decide what everything else should look like.

The Biggest Problem: You Have to Bring Everything Else

There is, however, one obvious catch. The Mac mini is just the computer. There is no monitor. No keyboard. No mouse. No webcam. No speakers beyond the built-in speaker.

That can be an advantage if you already have a great setup. But if you’re starting from scratch, the actual cost of building a complete desktop system can climb quickly.

This is why the Mac mini makes the most sense for a particular type of buyer: someone who wants a powerful desktop computer but doesn’t want Apple to dictate the rest of the setup.

Who Should Buy the Mac mini M4?

Students — It’s small, quiet and powerful enough for years of everyday academic work, programming, research and creative applications.

Developers — The M4’s CPU performance, unified memory architecture and macOS development environment make the Mac mini particularly interesting for programmers.

Creators — Video editors, designers, photographers and music producers can take advantage of Apple’s media engines and GPU performance.

AI and Tech Enthusiasts — The combination of Apple silicon and the Neural Engine makes the Mac mini an interesting machine for developers experimenting with AI applications and local workflows.

People Building a Minimal Desk Setup — If your goal is a clean, uncluttered workspace, the Mac mini is difficult to ignore.

The Mac mini M4 is a fascinating example of how far desktop computing has come. Apple has essentially taken a computer that would traditionally require a large chassis and compressed it into a 5-inch square box. But the impressive part isn’t simply that Apple made the Mac mini smaller. It’s that the company made it smaller without making it feel like a compromise.
