What Happened at Apple WWDC 2026 — Day 1

The keynote Apple has owed us for two years just landed. Here’s everything.

There’s a particular kind of anticipation that builds around an Apple keynote when the leaks have been loud and the pressure has been real. Walking into WWDC 2026 — Apple’s 37th annual Worldwide Developers Conference — everyone already knew the headline: Siri was getting a brain transplant.

What nobody quite knew was the scale of it, and how far Apple was willing to go.

Monday, June 8 opened with Tim Cook taking the stage at Apple Park. It would be his last WWDC keynote as CEO. He steps down August 31, handing the role to hardware engineering chief John Ternus on September 1 — a transition that loomed quietly over every announcement.

After the pleasantries, Craig Federighi laid out three pillars for 2026: platform performance, trust and safety, and a major push forward on Apple Intelligence. “We believe privacy in AI is non-negotiable,” he said, before spending the next two hours proving Apple means it differently than the rest of Silicon Valley.

The Main Event: Siri AI

This was always going to be the story. Apple’s overhaul of Siri — now officially branded Siri AI — is the most significant reimagining of the assistant in its fifteen-year history.

The new Siri understands what’s on your screen. It knows your personal context across apps. It can navigate third-party apps on your behalf, pull directions from an Instagram post, manage multi-step tasks without being handed off to another service, and hold a genuine back-and-forth conversation.

Apple VP Mike Rockwell described it plainly: “Siri is now a profoundly more capable assistant that helps you find what you need and gets more done. It’s also more conversational, so you can go back and forth like never before and get detailed, engaging answers.”

Alongside the upgraded intelligence, Apple introduced a standalone Siri app that manages persistent, conversational chat history synced across all your Apple devices. That puts Apple squarely in the same lane as ChatGPT and Claude in terms of user experience — except Siri lives inside the operating system itself.

The Google Deal

This is the one that raised eyebrows across the industry. Apple confirmed a deep partnership with Google, using the Gemini family of models to power its next-generation Apple Foundation Models. Reports put the deal at roughly $1 billion annually for access to a custom 1.2 trillion parameter Gemini LLM.

For context: Apple building on a competitor’s AI infrastructure is historically unprecedented. It signals clearly that Apple’s own in-house models were not where they needed to be, and that Tim Cook’s final act as CEO included making the pragmatic call to catch up fast rather than wait for internal balance.

iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS Golden Gate

No hardware was announced. This was a pure software event.

iOS 27 emphasized speed and stability over visual drama — faster app launches, quicker AirDrop transfers, optimized file browsing — alongside a customizable Liquid Glass transparency slider. Deep Siri AI integration threads through the whole OS.

macOS 27 Golden Gate — named after the strait connecting San Francisco Bay to the Pacific — is symbolically the end of the Intel era. This version drops support for Intel-based Macs entirely. Design-wise, it brings colored sidebar icons back, edge-to-edge sidebars, and Siri AI integrated directly into Spotlight search.

watchOS 27 made the steepest compatibility cut in Apple Watch history — Series 8, Ultra 1, and SE 2 are all out.

visionOS 27 brings a floating 3D Siri orb you can place anywhere in spatial space and activate just by looking at it.

What It All Means

After two years of promising a better Siri and delivering updates that didn’t quite land, Apple shipped. The Gemini partnership may sting the pride of some Apple loyalists, but the result — an assistant that actually understands context, remembers conversations, and lives natively in the OS — is what users have been asking for since 2011.

The AI race just got a third serious contender with two billion devices in the wild.
